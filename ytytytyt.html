The marbled water monitor (Varanus marmoratus), also known commonly as the Philippine water monitor, is a large species of monitor lizard in the family Varanidae. The species is endemic to the Philippines.

Specimen Overview

What is the Marbled Water Monitor?

The Marbled Water Monitor is a powerful, heavy-bodied reptile characterized by its elongated neck, sharp curved claws, and powerful muscular legs. It features a distinctive dark grey to brownish-black body adorned with intricate yellow and pale green marbled patterns across its back and limbs.

As an exceptionally agile swimmer and climber, it possesses a long, laterally flattened tail that acts like a rudder underwater. Its deeply forked tongue continuously flicks to scent-track prey, while its keen eyesight makes it one of the top cold-blooded predators in Philippine riparian ecosystems.

Ecology & Surroundings

Natural Habitats

Marbled water monitors thrive in aquatic-rich environments across the Philippine archipelago.

Mangroves & Estuaries

Frequently found navigating brackish waters, coastal mangrove forests, and tidal creeks.

Rivers & Freshwater Swamps

Utilizes inland freshwater streams, rivers, and flooded wetlands as primary hunting grounds.
